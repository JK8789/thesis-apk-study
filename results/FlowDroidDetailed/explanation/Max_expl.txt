2. Most interesting places in the MAX XML

These are the locations I would treat as the most useful for the thesis discussion.

1. one.me.android.deeplink.LinkInterceptorActivity.handleCallIntent(...)
This is the clearest externally meaningful location in the visible MAX XML. The result shows a deep-link interception component forwarding an incoming intent into startActivity(...). Even without proving misuse, this is security-relevant because deep-link entry points are externally influenced by design.

2. one.me.android.calls.a and one.me.calls.impl.service.f
These two locations appear repeatedly in activity-launch results. The important point is not the raw leak count, but the repeated pattern: incoming intent state, extras, or related runtime context influence subsequent call-related navigation. This suggests a substantial internal call-control layer.

3. ru.ok.tamtam.android.util.share.b
This class appears in both ContentResolver.update(...) and ContentResolver.insert(...) paths. Provider-backed writes are more interesting than generic logging because they indicate structured state manipulation through Android content-provider interfaces.

4. sn0.b
Like the TamTam share class, sn0.b appears in both insert and update results. The obfuscated name limits semantic interpretation, but repeated provider writes still make it a relevant place for manual inspection.

5. Media-handling paths in Facebook image pipeline
The MediaMetadataRetriever.setDataSource(FileDescriptor) and ExifInterface(FileDescriptor) results are among the clearest functionally meaningful MAX paths. They support the presence of image, file, or media-processing logic in the distributed APK.

6. Analytics and telemetry points
com.my.tracker.core.Tracer and ru.ok.android.externcalls.analytics.internal.log.DefaultCallAnalyticsLogger show that analytics and call-event instrumentation are present, but the currently visible paths mostly end in local logging sinks rather than in clearly external network sinks.
