3. Provider writes and updates: results 4, 5, 14, 43, 56, 62
These are the most interesting storage-like flows in the set because they hit ContentResolver.insert() or update(). Right now the tainted source is the resolver handle, not clearly a personal field, but these methods are still the best candidates for checking what data is actually persisted or passed to a provider.

4. Media/photo/video workflows: results 12, 22, 39, 51, 68, 71, 72
These correspond to take-photo, preview-photo, preview-video, media-player, and file-descriptor/media operations. Since Sberbank declares collection of photos/files and has camera/storage permissions, these flows may later help you connect declared functionality with actual media handling. They are not suspicious by themselves, but they are relevant to declared behavior.

5. Merchant permission and scanner flows: results 55 and 59
GrantMerchantDataPermissionsActivity and BaseScannerFragment are semantically useful names. They may help you connect manifest permissions, document/media capture, and merchant-related workflows.

6. Dialer / telephony-adjacent flow: result 45
DialerActivity.onCreate uses Intent.getData() to launch another activity. Because Sberbank requests call-related permissions, this is worth keeping for later comparison, even though the current result is still just IPC/navigation.
